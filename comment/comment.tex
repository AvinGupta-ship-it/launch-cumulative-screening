\section{Commenter and docket}

I am an independent researcher submitting this comment on the scope and content of the PEIS during the scoping period, which the FAA extended to December 7, 2026 (91 FR 72611). The comment responds to the FAA's invitation to ``submit comments on alternatives and impacts and on relevant information, studies, or analyses with respect to the proposed agency action'' (91 FR 65289, DATES). The notice states that the PEIS is being prepared under FAA Order 1050.1G (91 FR 65289), which took effect on June 30, 2025; the noise guidance cited below is the FAA's 1050.1F Desk Reference, Version 2, Chapter 11 (February 2020), the published noise chapter, which the FAA states is being updated to correspond with Order 1050.1G. The comment is based on a computational screening study that uses only public documents in the FAA's own record: the notice of intent, the FAA scoping presentation, the 2022 Programmatic Environmental Assessment, the 2025 Final Tiered Environmental Assessment and its noise appendix, the 2025 Mitigated FONSI/ROD, the 2026 Tiered EA, and the Desk Reference. The study, its code and its inputs are reproducible from the technical attachment. The study does not estimate the actual impacts of the Proposed Action; it examines how the metrics used in the existing record behave when the number of operations increases from 25 to 100 to 456 launches per year, and it identifies analyses and disclosures that would allow the PEIS to characterize that increase. Every recommendation below is a request for analysis, disclosure or clarification in the PEIS. Levels quoted at named communities are screening values from circularized FAA footprints, not predictions.

\section{Summary of recommendations}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0pt]
\item \textbf{Noise and noise-compatible land use.} Report, alongside DNL and CDNL, metrics from each aggregation class at named receptors: events above fixed $L_{A\max}$ levels, days per year with at least one such event, night-time counts, and sonic-boom counts above 1, 2, 4 and 6~psf. Energy-averaged metrics change predictably with count and night share; day-count metrics saturate at the proposed cadence (Attachment Section 5, Figures 1 and 2, Table 5).
\item \textbf{Noise.} Disclose the night-share assumption separately from the count. Of the \valCfTotalTwoFiveFourFiveSixDb~dB change in DNL between 25 launches with 3 at night and 456 with half at night, \valCfCountTermTwoFiveFourFiveSixDb~dB is due to the count and \valCfNightTermZeroOneTwoZeroFiveDbOneDp~dB to the night share; present DNL contours with and without the 50 percent night assumption.
\item \textbf{Noise; biological resources; coastal resources.} Analyze the temporal distribution of operations, not only annual totals: the weekday-only restriction in Phase 2, campaign-style clustering, and the spacing of landings. Interval metrics (days with events, longest undisturbed interval, weekend daylight availability) depend on it; count-based metrics do not (Attachment Section 5).
\item \textbf{Section 4(f) (49 U.S.C. 303), Section 6(f) (54 U.S.C. 200305(f)(3)), coastal resources, land use, socioeconomics.} State the per-operation access-restriction duration assumed for launches, landings, static fires and scrubs, and show that the 2,000 hours per year the PEIS will analyze is consistent with the proposed cadence: at 456 launches per year it is \valBudgetPerLaunchTwoZeroZeroZeroNFourFiveSixH~hours per launch, against 7.75 to 24.5 hours recorded for Flights 1 to 4 (Attachment Section 6, Figure 3). Report closure hours by weekday, weekend, holiday and season.
\item \textbf{Biological resources.} For disturbance-sensitive species in the adjacent refuge and critical habitat, analyze the fraction of time in a disturbed state and the availability of undisturbed intervals as functions of recovery time, and disclose the recovery times assumed and their sources. Whether per-event and cumulative assessments agree depends on the product of event rate and recovery time, unmeasured at this site (Attachment Section 7, Figure 4).
\item \textbf{Noise; biological resources.} Count landings of vehicles launched from other sites (up to 912 per year regardless of launch origin) explicitly in event, boom and day-count metrics; they change these metrics without changing the launch count (Attachment Section 5).
\item \textbf{All resource areas.} Include targeted sensitivity analyses: night share and vehicle configuration for noise metrics; closure practice and anomaly frequency for access metrics; recovery times for biological coverage metrics (Attachment Section 8, Figure 5).
\item \textbf{Alternatives.} Evaluate Phase 1 (100 launches per year) as a distinct alternative and consider an alternative with a cap on night operations; the metrics above distinguish these from Phase 2, and the notice identifies only the Proposed Action and No Action.
\item \textbf{Cumulative effects; air quality.} Disclose the combined annual stratospheric emissions of water vapor, carbon dioxide, nitrogen oxides and black carbon from Starship-Super Heavy operations at Starbase, Launch Complex 39A and Space Launch Complex 37, with the emission indices and stratospheric fractions assumed, since measured indices for methane-oxygen engines have not been published (Attachment Section 9).
\item \textbf{Record.} Reconcile three inconsistencies in the 2025 Tiered EA: the SEL contour extents stated in the text (8 and 15 miles) differ from the Appendix B maps (about 15 and 27 miles over land); the 2 and 1~psf Super Heavy landing boom extents are 27 and 28 miles in the text and 28 and 27 miles in Appendix B; and some structural-damage contours are labeled as A-weighted. Publish the modeled single-event contours as data tables (Attachment Section 2).
\end{enumerate}

\section{The cadence increase as described in the notice}

The notice states that SpaceX is currently authorized to conduct up to 25 launches and 50 landings per year; that Phase 1 would include up to 100 launches and 200 landings at any day or time; that Phase 2 would include up to 456 launches and 912 landings, with launches Monday through Friday only; that the landing thresholds apply regardless of launch origin; and that for the PEIS analysis half of launches and landings are assumed to occur at night (10 p.m. to 7 a.m.) (91 FR 65290). The FAA scoping presentation states, on slide 13, that the PEIS will analyze up to 2,000 hours per year of operational public access restrictions. The existing record (2025 Tiered EA and FONSI/ROD) was built from single-event footprints for one launch, one landing of each vehicle and one static fire; from annual emission totals at 25 launches; from a per-operation deluge volume; and from annual caps of 500 hours for operations and 300 hours for anomalies on access restrictions. Its cumulative noise analysis used DNL for 25 launches with 3 at night and CDNL for 44 daytime booms.

The question this comment addresses is which metrics, analyses and alternatives allow the PEIS to characterize the change from that record to the proposed cadence. The question is methodological: how each metric aggregates over events determines whether the per-event construction remains informative when the frequency increases by a factor of 18.

\section{Analysis}

\subsection{Aggregation classes}

The technical attachment classifies the metrics in the record by their aggregation operator. Additive metrics (water volume, emissions) scale linearly with the count. Energy-averaged metrics (DNL, CDNL) increase by $10\log_{10}$ of the count ratio plus a night-share term. Number-above metrics (events above a level, booms above an overpressure) scale linearly. Exceedance-day metrics (days with at least one event) cannot exceed the number of eligible days and saturate. Coverage metrics (fraction of time a receptor is disturbed) saturate once events arrive faster than the receptor recovers. Interval metrics (longest undisturbed gap, availability of a window of given length) and access-availability metrics (weekend and holiday daylight hours not under closure) depend on the spacing of events. The attachment gives the closed forms (Table 3) and verifies the simulation against them.

\subsection{Verification against the FAA record}

Using the FAA's own single-event contours digitized from Appendix B of the 2025 Tiered EA and the Desk Reference conversion from overpressure to C-weighted level, the screening model was compared with the quantities the EA reports for its 25-launch scenario (Attachment Section 4, Table 4). The annual emission totals and the deluge volume are reproduced exactly. The modeled DNL 65~dB overland radius (\valVerifDnlSixFiveDbContourExtentMiles~miles) matches the radius digitized from the EA's own DNL map (2.90 miles) to \valVerifDnlSixFiveVsDigitizedMiles~mile, and the 75~dB radius matches to \valVerifDnlSevenFiveVsDigitizedMiles~mile; the 65~dB value is \valVerifDnlSixFiveBelowLowEndPct~percent below the low end of the 3 to 3.5 miles stated in the EA text, because the text describes the longer directional extent and the model uses circular footprints. The modeled CDNL 60~dB extent (\valVerifCdnlSixZeroDbContourExtentMiles~miles) is \valVerifCdnlSixZeroAboveStatedPct~percent above the ``approximately 5 miles'' stated in the EA, which is given without a contour map; the comparison is limited by the rounding of that value and by the circular boom footprint. The study's verification criterion, reproduction of the EA's reported quantities within transcription and digitization tolerance, is met for the emissions, deluge and DNL quantities and only to the precision of the stated value for CDNL. The scaling results below therefore start from the agency's own cumulative-noise construction, with the CDNL comparison carried as a stated limitation.

\subsection{Scaling results}

With the FAA's per-event values and half of operations at night, between 25 and 456 launches per year (Attachment Table 5, Figure 1; Poisson timing on all days unless stated, with the weekday-only Phase 2 value beside it):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=1pt]
\item DNL at the nearest edge of Port Isabel rises from \valEOneNTwoFiveLdnPortisabel~to \valEOneNFourFiveSixLdnPortisabel~dB, a rise of \valEOneLdnPortisabelRiseTwoFiveFourFiveSixDb~dB at a fixed 50 percent night share against the \valCfCountTermTwoFiveFourFiveSixDb~dB closed-form count term; the number of events above 100~dBA $L_{A\max}$ there and the number of booms above 2~psf scale linearly, as expected. These are screening values from circularized footprints, not predictions.
\item The number of days per year with an event above 90~dBA at the nearest edge of South Padre Island is \valEOneSimDaysNineZeroSouthPadreIslandNearestEdge~at 456 launches under the all-days model (\valETwoWeekdayDaysNineZeroSpi~under the weekday-only model), against \valEOneNTwoFiveDaysNineZeroSpi~at 25; linear extrapolation overstates the all-days value by \valEOneRelerrPctDaysNineZeroSouthPadreIslandNearestEdge~percent (Figure~\ref{fig:cdays}). The weekday-only value exceeds the 250 business days because Starship landings drawn up to three days after launch fall on weekends. At the proposed cadence nearly every eligible day has an event, and the characterizing quantity becomes the number of events per exposed day.
\item The fraction of weekend and holiday daylight hours free of access restrictions falls from \valEOneNTwoFiveWeekendAvailPct~percent at 25 launches to \valEOneNFourFiveSixWeekendAvailPct~percent at 456 under the all-days model (\valETwoWeekdayWeekendAvailPct~percent under the weekday-only model), under hypothetical closure parameters bracketing the record; this quantity has no per-operation analogue.
\item Allowing landings of vehicles launched elsewhere up to 912 per year raises booms above 1~psf at the Port Isabel edge from \valEOneExtBoomOnePortisabelZero~to \valEOneExtBoomOnePortisabelOne~per year while changing DNL by \valEOneExtLdnDeltaDb~dB.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Clustering and the weekday restriction}

At a fixed 456 launches per year, Poisson, weekday-only renewal and campaign-style timing give the same additive, energy-averaged and number-above metrics but different interval metrics: days with an event above 90~dBA at the South Padre Island edge of \valETwoPoissonDaysNineZeroSpi~(Poisson), \valETwoWeekdayDaysNineZeroSpi~(weekday-only) and \valETwoCampaignDaysNineZeroSpi~(campaign); weekend daylight availability of \valETwoPoissonWeekendAvailPct, \valETwoWeekdayWeekendAvailPct~and \valETwoCampaignWeekendAvailPct~percent; and longest undisturbed gaps at the habitat receptor within a 1 March to 31 August season window of \valETwoPoissonLongestGapHabitatH, \valETwoWeekdayLongestGapHabitatH~and \valETwoCampaignLongestGapHabitatH~hours. The ranking of cadence levels does not change with the temporal model, but the magnitudes of the interval metrics do.

\subsection{Closure-cap consistency}

With the mean of the per-launch access-restriction hours recorded for Flights 1 to 4 (\valRecordMeanLaunchClosureH~hours) and hypothetical allocations for other events, expected annual hours including anomaly response (hypothetical anomaly probability 0.02 per launch, 48 hours each) are \valEFourRecordFlightsOneToFourNOneZeroZeroClosureH~at 100 launches and \valEFourRecordFlightsOneToFourNFourFiveSixClosureH~at 456 launches per year under the all-days model (\valEFourRecordWeekdayPhaseTwoNFourFiveSixClosureH~under the weekday-only model), and the probability of exceeding 2,000 hours at 456 launches is \valEFourRecordFlightsOneToFourNFourFiveSixPexceedTwoZeroZeroZero. The caps imply per-launch budgets of \valBudgetPerLaunchTwoZeroZeroZeroNFourFiveSixH~hours (2,000~h) and \valBudgetPerLaunchFiveZeroZeroNFourFiveSixH~hours (500~h) at 456 launches, against 7.75 hours for the shortest recorded launch (Figure~\ref{fig:cbudget}). These are not predictions of actual closures; they show that the consistency of the 2,000-hour assumption with the proposed cadence rests on a per-launch duration the PEIS can state and support.

\subsection{Disturbance coverage}

At the piping plover critical-habitat receptor, the disturbed fraction of a season follows the closed form $1 - \exp(-\lambda E[\tau])$ in the event rate $\lambda$ and mean recovery time $\tau$. The per-event sum $\lambda\tau$ overstates the simulated coverage by more than 10 percent for recovery times of about \valEFiveWeekdayTauOneZeroPctMin~minutes or longer at 456 launches per year under both the all-days and the weekday-only models, through the saturation of the closed form and the pairing of launches with the landings that follow them (at a one-hour recovery time the overstatement is \valEFivePoissonOverTauOneHPct~and \valEFiveWeekdayOverTauOneHPct~percent respectively); extrapolating instead from the coverage simulated at 25 launches overstates the all-days value at 456 by \valEOneRelerrPctDisturbedPipingPloverCriticalHabitatNearPadOneDp~percent (Attachment Table 5). With a one-hour recovery time the disturbed fraction is \valEOneNFourFiveSixDisturbedHabitat~(all days) and \valEFiveWeekdayDisturbedTauOneH~(weekday-only); with one day it is \valEFivePoissonDisturbedTauTwoFourH~and \valEFiveWeekdayDisturbedTauTwoFourH. Recovery times for the species and stressors at this site have not been published; the results are response surfaces in two unknown parameters.

\subsection{Sensitivity}

Total-order Sobol indices over sixteen inputs and the random seed show that at 456 launches per year the noise metrics are controlled by the vehicle level offset and night share, the boom counts and exposure days by the share of landings from other sites, with campaign clustering next for exposure days, CDNL by night share and the boom scale factor, the disturbed fraction by the recovery time, and the closure and availability metrics by the per-launch closure duration, anomaly frequency and scrub frequency (Attachment Section 8, Figure 5). These are the inputs on which PEIS sensitivity analyses would be most informative.

\section{Recommendations tied to resource areas and PEIS analyses}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=2pt]
\item \textit{Noise and noise-compatible land use (FAA Order 1050.1G; 14 CFR part 150 compatibility guidelines; 1050.1F Desk Reference chapter 11 supplemental metrics).} Report number-above, exceedance-day, night-count and boom-count metrics at named receptors (Port Isabel, South Padre Island, Laguna Vista, Laguna Heights, Brownsville, Boca Chica Village, Isla Blanca Park, Boca Chica State Park) for each cadence level, alongside DNL and CDNL. Evidence: Attachment Figure 1 and Table 5; the model reproduces the FAA's 2025 DNL contour radii and emission totals (Attachment Section 4).
\item \textit{Noise.} Present DNL and CDNL contours for each cadence with and without the 50 percent night assumption and state the count and night-share terms separately. Evidence: Attachment Figure 2 and the closed form in Table 3, which the simulation reproduces to within \valEThreeNightCfMaxabsdiffDbOneDp~dB.
\item \textit{Noise; biological resources; coastal resources.} Describe and analyze the temporal distribution of operations (weekday restriction, campaign clustering, landing spacing) and report interval metrics under it. Evidence: Attachment Section 5, where three timing models at the same count give exposure days from \valETwoCampaignDaysNineZeroSpi~to \valETwoPoissonDaysNineZeroSpi~per year.
\item \textit{Section 4\textup{(f)} (49 U.S.C. 303) and Section 6\textup{(f)} (54 U.S.C. 200305(f)(3)); coastal resources; land use; socioeconomics.} Disclose the per-operation closure durations assumed, demonstrate consistency with 2,000 hours per year at 456 launches, and report closure hours by weekday, weekend, holiday and season. Evidence: Attachment Figure 3, built from the four recorded per-launch durations and hypothetical parameters bracketing them.
\item \textit{Biological resources (ESA Section 7 consultation; refuge compatibility).} Analyze disturbed-time fraction and undisturbed-interval availability as functions of recovery time for species of concern, and disclose the recovery times and sources assumed. Evidence: Attachment Figure 4; the results are conditional response surfaces, not predictions.
\item \textit{Noise; biological resources.} Count landings of externally launched vehicles explicitly in all event-based metrics. Evidence: Attachment Section 5, where up to 912 external landings raise booms above 1~psf at the Port Isabel edge from \valEOneExtBoomOnePortisabelZero~to \valEOneExtBoomOnePortisabelOne~per year.
\item \textit{All resource areas.} Include the targeted sensitivity analyses identified in Attachment Figure 5.
\item \textit{Alternatives (FAA Order 1050.1G reasonable alternatives).} Evaluate Phase 1 as a distinct alternative and an alternative with a night-operations cap. Evidence: the metrics in Attachment Table 5 and Figure 2 distinguish these cases.
\item \textit{Cumulative effects; air quality.} Disclose combined stratospheric emissions across Starship sites with assumptions stated. Evidence: Attachment Section 9, where the fleet black-carbon interval spans an order of magnitude because the emission indices are unmeasured.
\item \textit{Record.} Reconcile the SEL contour statements and the boom extents with the Appendix B maps and text, and publish single-event contours as tables. Evidence: Attachment Section 2.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Limitations}

The screening study uses the FAA's existing per-operation values for the vehicle configuration assessed in 2024; it does not model future configurations except through labeled hypothetical scaling factors. Operational patterns within the stated caps, species-specific recovery times, and per-event closure durations other than the four recorded launches are unknown and are represented by labeled hypothetical ranges. The study does not estimate the actual impacts of the Proposed Action, and nothing in this comment asserts that any impact, harm or benefit will occur at any location. The results describe the behavior of metrics under stated assumptions and identify the analyses and disclosures that would resolve the uncertainties.

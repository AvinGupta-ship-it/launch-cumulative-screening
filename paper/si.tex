\section{Derivations of the closed forms}

\paragraph{Energy-averaged level.} With $N$ events of identical sound exposure level $L_{AE}$ at a receptor, a fraction $f_n$ at night, $10^{L_{dn}/10} = N[(1-f_n) + 10 f_n]\,10^{L_{AE}/10}/T_s$, so that the change between two cadences and night fractions is $10\log_{10}(N_2/N_1) + 10\log_{10}[(1+9f_{n,2})/(1+9f_{n,1})]$. With several event types the same decomposition holds for each type and the total is the energy sum.

\paragraph{C-weighted day-night level.} The Desk Reference form $\mathrm{CDNL} = \mathrm{CSEL} + 10\log_{10}(N_d + 10N_n) - 49.4$ applies to daily counts; with annual counts the daily counts are $N/365$ and the expression equals $10\log_{10}[\sum_i 10^{(\mathrm{CSEL}_i + 10\,\mathbb{1}_{n,i})/10}/(365\times 86400)]$ up to the rounding of $10\log_{10}(86400) = 49.37$ to 49.4.

\paragraph{Exceedance days.} If $N_L$ exceeding events are placed independently and uniformly over $D$ eligible days, the number falling on a given day is approximately Poisson with mean $N_L/D$, so the probability that the day has at least one event is $1 - e^{-N_L/D}$ and the expected number of such days is $D(1 - e^{-N_L/D})$. The bound $D$ is 365 for unrestricted timing and the number of business days (\num{250} in the scenario year) for weekday-only launches; landings and static fires are not restricted.

\paragraph{Coverage.} Events arrive as a Poisson process of rate $\lambda$ and each starts a disturbed interval of random length $\tau_i$ with mean $E[\tau]$. The union of intervals is the busy period of an $M/G/\infty$ queue; the number of intervals covering a fixed time is Poisson with mean $\lambda E[\tau]$, so the stationary probability of being disturbed is $1 - \exp(-\lambda E[\tau])$ \citep{daley2003,hall1988}. The season window over which coverage and interval metrics are evaluated is 1 March to 31 August (days 59 to 243 of the scenario year), an illustrative six-month window. Linear extrapolation gives $\lambda E[\tau]$, which exceeds the exact value by $\lambda E[\tau] - 1 + e^{-\lambda E[\tau]} \approx (\lambda E[\tau])^2/2$ for small arguments.

\paragraph{Undisturbed windows.} With fixed recovery time $\tau$ an undisturbed window of length $w$ requires a gap of at least $w + \tau$ between consecutive events. For a Poisson process the gaps are exponential; with approximately $\lambda S$ gaps in a season of length $S$ the probability that none exceeds $w + \tau$ is approximately $\exp\{-\lambda S e^{-\lambda(w+\tau)}\}$, a Gumbel-type asymptotic that is accurate when $\lambda S$ is large. The simulation does not rely on this approximation.

\section{Digitization method and residuals}

Pages of the noise appendix were rasterized at 300 dpi with PyMuPDF. The map frame was located from the long horizontal rules, the legend swatches from saturated horizontal segments in the legend box in row-major order, and the scale bar from the longest dark run shorter than the box border (\num{58} pixels per mile on the zoomed maps). Pixels within an RGB distance of 55 of a swatch color were assigned to that contour after removing solid fills (pixels whose $15\times15$ neighbourhood was mostly matching). The pad was the centroid of the innermost contour. Pixels were binned into 16 azimuth sectors; the median radius per sector and the median over the overland sector (240--360\si{\degree}) are stored in \texttt{data/processed/digitized\_contours.csv}. Table S2 reports the residuals of the propagation law; the hybrid evaluator reproduces every digitized radius exactly by construction. The text extents that disagree with the maps are the launch SEL 100 dBA (8 miles in the text, \num{14.8} miles on the map) and 90 dBA (15 miles in the text, \num{27} miles on the map) contours.

\section{Literature-search protocol}

The gap statement rests on the queries recorded in \texttt{results/summary/literature\_search.md}, run against the OpenAlex works endpoint on the date recorded there with relevance sorting and the first 25 results inspected. Query strings are written in Boolean form so that they can be rerun on Web of Science or Scopus, which were not available. No retrieved work combined a calendar-resolved operations model with the aggregation classes of launch-site NEPA metrics; the closest works treat single-event launch acoustics, global rocket emissions, or disturbance responses to individual stimuli.
